How timing works

Built so clubs can trust lap times with the hardware they already have: the APEX lap counter, Trackmate, and Arduinos running Race Coordinator’s program.

At a glance

Microsecond timing

With the APEX lap counter every sensor is timestamped by a hardware interrupt, so a pack crossing together is timed as precisely as a single car — even with bouncing contacts.

Every board corrected

A typical Arduino can drift 30 ms on a 6-second lap. APEX takes that to 0.01 ms per lap with any supported counter, and 0.005 ms with the APEX lap counter.

Nothing lost

Every lap is timed on the lap counter and saved the moment it happens. A busy computer or a power cut doesn’t throw times away.

The hidden error

Arduino Uno boards use a ceramic resonator accurate to about ±0.5 %. On a 6-second lap that is up to 30 ms; on a 3-minute heat, up to 0.9 s. Lap times are shown to a thousandth, so the drift is visible.

How APEX fixes it

The computer’s clock is crystal-controlled and about a hundred times more accurate. APEX measures the lap counter’s clock against the computer continuously and scales every lap to real seconds. The correction is ready within the first couple of minutes, gets tighter the longer it runs, and is saved for the next meeting.
